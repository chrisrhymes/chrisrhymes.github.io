He opened his eyes in the darkness and looked down at his phone screen as it flashed into life. The piercing sound of the alert had woken him. Informing him to be ready. A bulletin notification popped up on the screen of the black rectangular device. The notification was always from the same app, the only app on the device, the only app that existed. As far as he knew, the only app to have ever existed.

The message was an official reminder. He hurriedly swiped the screen from bottom to top with the index finger of his right hand to unlock it. No matter the time of day he had to read the reminder within the allocated 60 seconds of receipt to avoid the punishment.

You had three strikes each day. Miss three notifications and there would be consequences. No excuses.

With each passing moment, the clock counted down. He read through the message, reading fast, whilst also memorising the content. Once he had confirmed the response of the message, it would be wiped from his phone. Disappearing without a trace.

If you forgot what the message said and broke the new law they would make an example of you. Always watching. He looked up briefly to the red light in the corner of his room. The camera was always recording.

Sweat dripped from his forehead, his heartbeat racing, he looked back to his phone as the seconds ticked away. The message was longer than usual, the wording harder to understand, like it was written by either an absolute genius or a madman, or perhaps a little of both.

The rules were getting more and more strict. More and more complex, layer upon layer of bureaucracy building forever upward. More and more people were being made examples of each day. More and more people feared the consequences if they didn’t follow the instructions to the letter.

He read the final word, giving his mind a few precious seconds to work out the full meaning and store the words away in his brain as best as he could.

The clock continued to decrease lower and lower, counting down each millisecond as it passed. He couldn’t wait any longer. He pressed the confirm button, the message disappeared from view, along with the timer.

A message appeared on the screen. “Thank you for confirming. Ensure you are quicker next time. It is everyone’s responsibility to follow the rules.”

He shut his eyes, seeing the words from the original message burnt into his eyelids from the bright screen in the darkness. He first learned about this trick from a friend, but the friend told one to many people and was removed from the population. That was before they had cameras everywhere and they still found out. Somehow.

Rereading the message one more time from his eyelids, the words slowly faded with each second that passed. He double checked its meaning and what he was no longer allowed to do.

Even though it was technically cheating, anything was better than the alternative. He needed all the help he could get. He didn’t want to become one of the disappeared. One of the people shown on the screen each day. Paraded before the gathered crowd of faithful followers in the capital, their names shown on screen, before each letter was deleted one letter at a time, indicating their identity was being erased from existence. Indicating that they were no longer part of society. They would then walk down the narrow walkway, the end shrouded in darkness, never to be seen again.

No one knew where they went. No one talked about it. No one dared ask.

He breathed deeply, trying to slow down his pulse, trying to calm his nerves. It didn’t work, it never did. The only way sleep would come to him was to let the emotion out. Tears filled his eyes and he sobbed, a pathetic mess of a human being, so worn down, all resistance drained away.

Eventually he stopped crying and drifted back off into an uneasy sleep, not wanting to sleep too deeply for fear of missing the next alert.
